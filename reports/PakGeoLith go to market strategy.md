# PakGeoLith should sell trust, not satellites

PakGeoLith has a real opening, but it is not the one the founder describes. The research found **no other Pakistani private company selling a packaged satellite and AI mineral-exploration service**. It also found that the belief that "nobody in the world offers this" is **false and easy to disprove**. Well-funded foreign firms already sell satellite-based exploration. One of them, Fleet Space, worked for Barrick at Reko Diq in 2024. Pakistan's space agency SUPARCO launched its own hyperspectral satellite (a satellite that splits light into hundreds of narrow colour bands to identify minerals) in October 2025. Free NASA and USGS imagery covers most of the country. What PakGeoLith can defend is the **bundle**: one atlas of geology, mineral occurrences and tenure (who holds which licence) across provinces, a mining-law register, licence-compliance reports, an Urdu/English AI assistant and transparent PKR prices. That bundle answers Pakistan's documented problems: titles that are hard to check, scattered government data and a trust gap in the gemstone trade. Most of the money will come from **domestic corporates moving into minerals, foreign investors and juniors, and lenders and law firms**, not from small mine owners on PKR 5,000 subscriptions. The marketplace only earns if it handles verification, assay (lab testing of grade) and escrow (a neutral party holding payment until both sides deliver), and charges a percentage rather than a flat PKR 5,000 per lot. Government work runs province by province, starting with no-cost MoUs and pilots. The most urgent fact is the calendar: **PMIF26, the government's flagship minerals investment forum, opens in Islamabad on 12–13 November 2026, nine days from today (3 November 2026)**. If you are thinking of joining, check traction, data rights, report quality and the founder's willingness to drop the "only one in the world" claim. Those four things matter more than the technology.

## Plain-language glossary for a newcomer

The mining world uses a lot of shorthand. The terms below come up throughout this report.

| Term | What it means here |
|---|---|
| Tenure / title / licence / lease | The legal right to explore or mine a piece of land. Provinces grant these in Pakistan. "Tenure data" means maps of who holds which licence, where, and whether it is valid. |
| Cadastre | The official register and map of mining titles, like a land registry for mineral rights. |
| Exploration licence (EL) vs mining lease (ML) | An EL lets you search for minerals; an ML lets you dig and sell them. |
| Remote sensing | Studying the ground from satellites or aircraft. Multispectral sensors (Landsat, Sentinel-2, ASTER) see about a dozen colour bands; hyperspectral sensors (EMIT, HS-1, Tanager) see hundreds, which helps identify specific minerals. |
| Alteration mapping / targeting | Finding rock that hot fluids have chemically changed. This often surrounds ore deposits, so it suggests "targets" worth visiting on the ground. |
| Ground-truthing | Visiting the site to take samples and check whether the satellite interpretation was right. |
| Assay | A lab test that measures how much of a metal or mineral a sample contains (for example, % Cr2O3 in chromite ore). |
| JORC / NI 43-101; Competent or Qualified Person | International reporting codes (Australian and Canadian) for declaring mineral resources to investors. Only a certified professional can sign them. A satellite report is **not** one of these. |
| Junior | A small exploration company, usually listed on a stock exchange, that finds deposits and sells or partners them to bigger miners. |
| Major | A large producing mining company, such as Barrick or Rio Tinto. |
| SOE | State-owned enterprise, such as OGDCL or PMDC. |
| DFI | Development finance institution, such as the World Bank's IFC or the ADB. |
| Take rate | The share of each transaction's value that a marketplace keeps as its fee. |
| Disintermediation | Buyers and sellers meet on a platform, then cut the platform out of later deals. |
| Escrow | A neutral party holds the buyer's money until the goods are delivered and checked. |
| MoU | Memorandum of understanding: a non-binding statement of intent to cooperate. |
| JV / farm-in | Joint venture, a shared project. In a farm-in, one party earns a stake in another's licence by paying for exploration. |
| PPRA | Public Procurement Regulatory Authority: the rules for how government agencies buy services. |
| SIFC | Special Investment Facilitation Council: the high-level federal body that fast-tracks priority investment, including minerals. |
| GSP, PMDC, SUPARCO | Geological Survey of Pakistan, Pakistan Mineral Development Corporation (the state miner) and Pakistan's space agency. |
| PMIF | Pakistan Minerals Investment Forum, the government's annual flagship minerals conference. |

## Competitors exist worldwide; nobody yet bundles Pakistan this way

The founder's belief does not hold up. Satellite and AI mineral exploration is a crowded, heavily funded global industry. **KoBold Metals raised $537M at a $2.96B valuation in January 2025** ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/gates-backed-kobold-metals-raises-537m-in-funding-round-2025-01-06)). KoBold and Earth AI explore for their own account rather than selling reports. The vendors that do sell services are closer competitors. **Fleet Space raised USD 100M at a USD 525M valuation in December 2024** ([OTPP](https://otpp.com/en-ca/about-us/news-and-insights/2024/fleet-space-closes-usd-100m-series-d-with-usd-525m-valuation/)) and in July 2024 **deployed its ExoSphere system at Barrick's Reko Diq copper-gold project in Balochistan** ([Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/barrick-taps-fleet-spaces-technology-to-advance-copper-exploration-at-reko-diq/)). VRIFY markets its DORA software as the "world's first AI-assisted mineral discovery platform" ([Newswire](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/vrify-lands-12-5m-series-b-to-advance-dora-the-world-s-first-ai-assisted-mineral-discovery-platform-828597303.html)), which shows that "world's first" is a claim other companies make too. The closest match to PakGeoLith is **India's Farmonaut**. It sells tiered satellite mineral-detection reports online, quoted per project, positions them as support before a formal JORC or NI 43-101 study, and runs an affiliate programme ([Farmonaut](https://farmonaut.com/mining/farmonaut.com/satellite-based-mineral-detection)). Pixxel (India) and Planet (USA) sell hyperspectral imagery and analytics with mining use cases ([Pixxel](https://www.pixxel.space/blogs/introducing-aurora-pixxels-earth-observation-studio); [Planet](https://www.planet.com/pulse/unleash-the-power-of-hyperspectral-over-50-tanager-radiance-datasets-now-available-on-planet-s/)). The Dutch firm Fugro won a €10M Punjab government mineral-exploration contract back in 2017 ([GeoConnexion](https://geoconnexion.com/news/pakistan-government-chooses-fugro-for-extensive-minerals-exploration)).

A capable buyer can also get a great deal for free. **NASA's EMIT instrument has published free global maps of 10 surface minerals across arid regions** ([JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-sensor-produces-first-global-maps-of-surface-minerals-in-arid-regions/)), although its coverage of Pakistan was not confirmed. Landsat imagery, which once cost $400–4,000 per scene, is now free ([Farmonaut](https://farmonaut.com/?p=171882)). USGS and GSP have published a free digital geologic map of west-central Pakistan ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20111093)). Punjab runs an online mineral cadastre with e-auctions ([Punjab MMD](https://mnm.punjab.gov.pk/node/317)), and KP has a cadastre portal built on the Trimble/Spatial Dimension Landfolio system ([KP portal](https://portal.kpminerals.gov.pk/Portal/); [Trimble](https://landadmin.trimble.com/tag/egovernment)). At the cheap end, many Pakistan-based freelance geologists and GIS specialists sell mineral mapping on Upwork ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/geologists/pk/)).

The Pakistani state itself is the biggest long-term competitor, and also the biggest potential partner. **SUPARCO already lists "Geology & Mineral Prospection" as a service** ([SUPARCO](https://suparco.gov.pk/?p=3368)). A federal National Minerals Data Centre (NMDC) is being built as a GIS platform for investors and was demonstrated in Riyadh in January 2026 ([Pakistan Observer](https://pakobserver.net/national-minerals-data-centre-being-es-tablished-for-investors-facilitation-projects-planning/); [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=218132)). In 2024 GSP tendered for firms to carry out geological mapping using remote sensing ([GSP notice](https://gsp.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Notice-1-1-24.pdf)). That shows the government both does this work and buys it.

**Reconciling the HS-1 question.** The research notes disagree on whether SUPARCO's hyperspectral satellite HS-1 actually launched. The government-partnerships notes found only a September 2025 pre-launch announcement ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/09/22/pakistan-to-launch-hyperspectral-satellite-in-october-to-boost-mineral-mapping-and-agriculture-research/)) and flagged the launch as unconfirmed. The competitor notes cite three independent post-launch sources: Dawn, Pakistan's Foreign Ministry press release and SUPARCO's own programme page. All three report that **HS-1 launched on 19 October 2025 from Jiuquan, China**, with officials saying it would let Pakistan "independently map its mineral reserves" ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1950017/pakistan-launches-its-first-hyperspectral-satellite); [MOFA](https://mofa.gov.pk/press-releases/suparco-successfully-launches-pakistans-first-hyperspectral-satellite); [SUPARCO HS-1](https://suparco.gov.pk/major-programmes-satellite-programs/hs1.html)). The launch should be treated as confirmed; the gap in the other notes was a search gap, not contrary evidence. Three things remain unknown: **whether HS-1 is operating normally, whether its data is for sale, and at what price.** This matters for the pitch. Telling a Pakistani official that "nobody has this satellite system" a year after the government publicly launched one would damage PakGeoLith's credibility in the room where it most needs to be taken seriously.

So what is distinctive? The research supports a narrower claim: **"Pakistan's first integrated mineral-intelligence platform: geology, tenure, law and satellite targeting in one place, priced in PKR."** Global vendors publish no prices and sell through enterprise quotes ([VRIFY pricing](https://vrify.com/pricing)). Tenure data is split by province. Balochistan, the most important mineral province, has no public online cadastre that the research could find, and its 2025 Act, which mandated one, was **suspended by the Balochistan High Court in February 2026** ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/minerals-diplomacy-meets-market-reality-case-pakistan)). Reliable Balochistan tenure data would be PakGeoLith's most valuable asset. Yet, as noted in the earlier website review, **Balochistan is missing from PakGeoLith's own tenure coverage**. Closing that gap matters more than any satellite feature. The ranked sources of defensibility are a cleaned proprietary tenure and law dataset, a feedback loop from customers' field results, local compliance workflows, government relationships, and Urdu-language AI plus local sales. The satellite analytics alone are a commodity.

## Money sits with corporates, foreigners and financiers, not small miners

Pakistan's mining sector is small for its geology but politically prioritised. The Planning Minister put mineral exports at about **$2bn a year, mining at 2–3% of GDP, around 5,000 operating mines, only 40% of the country geologically mapped and 90% of exports unprocessed** ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1969522)). Other official sources cite about $3bn in exports with an $8bn target ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/16/pakistan-the-next-critical-minerals-destination/)); the difference probably comes from whether refined copper is counted. **KP alone has 4,917 mineral titles, up from 482 in 2010, with more than 500 applications pending** ([ARY News](https://arynews.tv/khawaja-asif-denies-military-seeking-mineral-licences-in-kp)). That suggests a national total of roughly 10,000–15,000 titles, an unverified estimate. Pakistan also has an estimated 400,000–515,000 artisanal and small-scale miners ([artisanalmining.org](https://artisanalmining.org/InventoryData/doku.php/country:pakistan)). These numbers show breadth, but the spending power is concentrated in a few places: Reko Diq, with **$8.83bn capex and over $2.6bn of IFC, ADB and US EXIM financing** ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/16/pakistan-the-next-critical-minerals-destination/)); the Chagai copper-gold belt; chromite; and the new critical-minerals deals.

The six customer types differ sharply in what they will pay for. Small licence holders and mine owners are the largest group but the most price-sensitive. KP mine owners are already protesting a 400% rise in surface rent and 765 mining areas made inaccessible by expanded forest boundaries ([APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1245381)). Domestic corporates and SOEs moving into hard-rock minerals have cash and are new to the sector, which makes them the best early enterprise buyers. **Mari took an 87.5% stake in Chagai licences and is farming out 49% to Fatima-linked Globacore** ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=186585); [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=221792)). PPL won conditional approval for exploration licence EL-331 in Chagai ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=216856)), and OGDCL is planning an antimony JV with PMDC ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=191118)). Foreign entrants face the largest information gap and are used to paying for data: US Strategic Metals, with a roughly $500M MoU with FWO; Nova Minerals; Saudi and Chinese groups ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/11/14/us-based-firm-eyes-investment-in-pakistans-mining-sector/amp/)). Lenders, insurers, law firms and consultants need title and environmental/social diligence around financed projects. The rest are buyers and traders (Chinese importers, gem dealers), government and academia.

### Who to sell subscriptions to

At PKR 5,000 (about USD 18) and PKR 10,000 (about USD 36) a year, PakGeoLith is priced **below retail mining-stock newsletters, which charge USD 199–399 a year** ([Gold Stock Data](https://www.goldstockdata.com/interviews/239-Interview-on-Mining-Stock-Daily.html)). Professional mining data costs far more. Mining Magazine charges £735–2,395 a year for up to three users ([Mining Magazine](https://miningmagazine.com/subscribe)), and S&P Capital IQ Pro is estimated at USD 12,000–30,000 per user per year ([CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/financial-data-terminals/sp-capital-iq/)). Simple arithmetic shows the problem: **1,000 paying subscribers at PKR 5,000 bring in only PKR 5M (about USD 18,000) a year**. That is not enough to pay a small team. The cheap tier should stay, because it suits students, geologists and small leaseholders and builds a user base, but it cannot carry the business. The research proposes a ladder (these are proposed prices, not market quotes). Keep a PKR 5,000 Explorer tier. Add a **Professional tier at PKR 25,000–40,000** for consultants and serious owners, and a **Business tier of up to five seats at PKR 120,000–180,000**, roughly matching IndiaMART's verified-supplier package of ₹50,000 a year ([IndiaMART](https://help.indiamart.com/?p=4684)). Add an **International Investor tier priced in dollars at USD 1,200–2,500 per seat** and **Enterprise/API licences at USD 10,000–40,000**, which sits below the per-seat terminal prices above. The website review also found a "Starter" tier mentioned on the site that does not appear on the plans page. Pricing needs to be consistent everywhere before any of this is marketed.

The target list for paid tiers follows from the segment analysis. Business seats should go to OGDCL, PPL, Mari, PMDC, FWO-linked contractors, Fatima/Globacore and cement groups that hold limestone leases. International seats should go to juniors and investors arriving through PMIF26, Riyadh and PDAC. Institutional seats should go to the law firms, banks and consultancies advising them; PMIF26's knowledge partners, Milbank, White & Case and Lazard, are exactly this type ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/28/pakistan-minerals-investment-forum-2026-pmif26-set-for-november-12-13-in-islamabad/)). Universities and government get discounted seats in exchange for credibility.

### Who to sell reports to

The **PKR 150k–1M satellite survey reports (about USD 540–3,600)** cost roughly what one to three days of a UK geological consultant cost at £400–1,200 a day ([Palsatech](https://palsatech.fi/?p=5833)). That is cheap for foreign clients and steep for small local leaseholders. The natural buyers are, first, corporates and SOEs deciding where to apply for or farm into licences in Chagai and elsewhere. Second come foreign juniors doing area selection before committing to Pakistan. Third come investors and lenders checking a licence before a deal. The research recommends keeping PKR prices locally and adding a **USD price list of roughly USD 3,000–15,000** for foreign clients, depending on area and deliverables (a proposal, not a benchmark). The **PKR 30k compliance report** suits a different buyer and should be sold around a transaction event: a sale, transfer, bank loan, dispute, or the 18-month window the new harmonised framework gives existing title holders to comply ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/2a6c8284/one-stop-shop-pakistan-unveils-harmonised-national-minerals-framework)). The research estimates that if 5–10% of an estimated 10,000–15,000 titles bought one report a year, revenue would be about **PKR 15–45M a year**. That is useful, but it confirms that enterprise and foreign buyers have to carry the business. A **USD 300–500 foreign price** for the same compliance report is justified. Overseas Pakistanis are a speculative but natural buyer of a "verify before you invest" pack. Roshan Digital Account inflows reached **$14.16B cumulatively by September 2026** ([APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1291269)), and a fake investment company in Gilgit-Baltistan took Rs240M from about 700 people ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=42866)). Remote investors are the people most exposed to that kind of fraud.

Report quality is now a sales issue. The sample T4 report reviewed earlier (PKR 950k, three blocks in Gilgit-Baltistan, looking for platinum-group metals, rare earths and tungsten) was **honest and methodologically careful but found no targets**. It was dated the same day the inputs arrived, while the site advertises a 20–30 day turnaround. It also had broken tables and a lot of unexplained jargon. A "no targets" result can be valuable, because it stops a client spending far more on fieldwork. But a client paying nearly a million rupees needs a one-page plain-language summary explaining that value. Turnaround dates also need to match the promise, or buyers will suspect templated output. Reports should state clearly that they are not JORC or NI 43-101 resource statements, and a partnership with a Competent Person firm would let PakGeoLith offer an upgrade path for international investors.

## A marketplace pays only if it owns verification, assay and escrow

The founder is right that buyers of minerals exist. The question is which minerals, and what the platform can control. **Copper** is the headline export: **copper exports to China passed $1bn in the first eight months of FY2025-26** ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/05/25/pakistans-copper-exports-to-china-cross-1-billion-in-8-months-amid-surge-in-processed-metal-trade)). But that trade is mostly processed metal from large state-linked and Chinese-operated projects, and copper ore and concentrate exports were only about $11M in 2025 ([Aaj](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330429078)). There is little copper for small sellers to list. **Chromite** fits a marketplace better: many small Balochistan producers, **$89.43M of exports to China in 2025**, but halving in the first half of the year on weak steel demand ([Aaj](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330429078)). It is a bulk commodity traded on grade, so it needs assay and logistics. Marble and construction stone are numerous but low-value, with about $5.4M of cut marble exported to China in 2025 ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/02/10/pakistans-becomes-chinas-3rd-largest-supplier-of-construction-stone-material/amp/)). **Gemstones** have the largest gap between potential and reality: **formal exports of about $5.8M a year** against a government target of $1bn within five years ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2628746/amp)). The trade runs informally through places like Peshawar's Namak Mandi, which has more than 700 shops ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/summer-2021-namak-mandi-a-pioneering-gemstone-market-in-pakistan)), and online buyers report Pakistani sellers reusing copied certificates ([Gem Society forum](https://forums.gemsociety.org/t/tourmalines-and-other-stones-from-pakistani-internet-sellers/8143)). The state-linked PGJDC also says it runs an online trading portal and gem exchanges ([PGJDC](https://pgjdc.org/about.php?cId=72)), and its live status needs checking.

Marketplaces around the world make money in three ways. Discovery platforms such as IndiaMART charge suppliers subscriptions and take no commission. IndiaMART has about **220,000 paying suppliers averaging ₹64,000 (about USD 730) a year** ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/indiamart-q1-profit-soars-35-yoy-to-inr-154-cr)). Auction operators take small percentages: India's MSTC charged a 0.2% service fee on Goa iron-ore auctions ([Goa DMG](https://dmg.goa.gov.in/PDFs/E-Auction/Clarification%20Regarding%20Base%20Price%20And%20Details%20Concerning%20The%20E-Auction%20On%2017-Feb-2014.pdf)). Commodity marketplaces generally run **take rates below 1%** ([Tidemark](https://www.tidemarkcap.com/vskp-chapter/marketplace-take-rates)), while more managed B2B marketplaces earn **3–15%** depending on the services they add ([Multiples.vc](https://docs.multiples.vc/coverage/b2b-marketplaces)). Full-stack traders like India's Metalbook buy and resell. That makes revenue look large, but Metalbook spent 1.03 rupees for every rupee of revenue ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/metalbooks-gross-revenue-crosses-rs-1300-cr-in-fy25-10618334)), so PakGeoLith should not hold inventory.

The main danger is disintermediation. A field experiment found that once trust builds, more deals move off the platform ([SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/390411)). Platforms keep users when they provide services that are hard to replicate on their own: payment terms, quality assurance, logistics and finance ([Hokodo](https://www.hokodo.co/resources/how-to-prevent-disintermediation-on-your-b2b-marketplace)). For PakGeoLith those services are **licence verification (only sellers with valid titles may list), assay coordination, sealed-sample chain of custody, standard grading per mineral, and escrow**. The success fee should apply only when those services are used. The **flat PKR 5,000-per-lot auction fee is far too low for valuable lots**. Gemfields' December 2025 emerald auction averaged about USD 550,000 per lot ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/gemfields-reports-254m-result-at-year-end-commercial-emerald-auction-2025-12-05)), so a USD 18 flat fee on lots like that earns almost nothing. The recommended structure is **"PKR 5,000 or 1–2.5% of the hammer price, whichever is greater"** for gemstone lots, **1–2% on escrow-settled deals and 0.5% on bulk minerals such as chromite**, a verified-seller membership in the Business tier, and a 10–20% mark-up on assay and inspection coordination.

Two practical constraints apply. **Pakistan has no escrow law**, and an IBA study describes escrow use as "limited and informal" ([IBA](https://ir.iba.edu.pk/sbselp/80)). PakGeoLith should therefore use a bank trustee account or a licensed payment provider such as Safepay ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=193897)) rather than hold client money itself, and use Raast for settlement. Commission income also attracts **12% advance withholding tax for filers under Section 233** ([PKRevenue](https://pkrevenue.com/advance-tax-rates-on-brokerage-commission-for-2025-26/)), plus provincial sales tax on services, which needs checking province by province. The website review found **auctions described as both live and not yet live**. That needs fixing immediately. In a market with documented certificate fraud, unclear claims about whether money can change hands are exactly what makes buyers cautious.

## Government deals start with provincial pilots, not national contracts

Government is PakGeoLith's most important partner and its most important competitor, so the order of approach matters. **Since the 18th Amendment, provinces own minerals.** They grant titles and collect royalties, and they have resisted federal control. The Balochistan High Court suspended the province's 2025 Act over alleged federal overreach ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/minerals-diplomacy-meets-market-reality-case-pakistan)), and on 25 October 2026 Gilgit-Baltistan's Chief Minister said minerals "should not be the exclusive domain of the federal government" ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2636772/mines-minerals-provincial-subjects-says-g-b-cm?amp=1)). At federal level, SIFC convenes and unblocks; for example, it helped AJK resume licensing ([Radio Pakistan](https://www.radio.gov.pk/20-05-2026/sifcs-efforts-revitalize-mining-sector-in-pakistan)). The Petroleum Division sets policy and hosts PMIF. GSP and SUPARCO own data. PMDC and OGDCL are state miners signing JVs. Researchers describe Pakistan's geological data as **"outdated, fragmented, or insufficiently accessible to investors"** and recommend a national data portal with tiered access ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/414308777_From_Uncertainty_to_Opportunity_Strengthening_the_Geological_Survey_Framework_for_Efficient_Mineral_Exploration_in_Pakistan)). Over 95% of mineral terrain is underexplored ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/minerals-diplomacy-meets-market-reality-case-pakistan)). That gap is PakGeoLith's opening, but the state is also trying to close it, through the NMDC, a UK-funded GSP digital-survey training programme ([24NewsHD](https://www.24newshd.tv/16-Apr-2026/new-uk-pakistan-partnership-will-bolster-pakistan-s-geological-survey)), and a China-facing "Pak-China E-Mining Platform" launched in January 2026 whose developer is unknown ([Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/pakistan-china-launch-e-mining-platform-sign-several-mous/)).

The research supports a three-step sequence. **Step one is a no-cost MoU or pilot.** The closest Pakistani model is SUPARCO's December 2025 MoU with the agri-tech firm E-Agri, in which a private company adds analysis to state satellite data ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/12/19/suparco-and-e-agri-sign-landmark-mou-to-revolutionize-pakistans-agriculture-with-space-driven-intelligence/amp/)). A PakGeoLith–SUPARCO MoU on HS-1 mineral analytics would turn the competitor into a data supplier. Similar pilots could digitise one district's legacy GSP reports or build an investor-facing layer on KP's cadastre. **Step two is an unsolicited proposal.** Since a January 2020 cabinet amendment, PPRA rules let any private party submit an unsolicited proposal, which a committee of at least three technical experts assesses (cited as Rule 37-A) ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/?p=526105)). PakGeoLith should expect the proposal to go to open tender, since favouritism disputes have arisen before ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2022/03/08/pakistan-posts-ict-digital-platform-project-tendering-process-raises-questions/)). Provincial projects follow provincial procurement rules. **Step three is a paying contract**: an annual data licence or white-label portal for a provincial licensing authority, proposed at PKR 5–25M a year with no benchmark behind that figure, or an investor data room for SIFC or PMIF.

Larger models have international precedents. Zambia paid a Canadian firm for a national airborne survey and will sell the data to investors "for a nominal fee" ([Zambia Monitor](https://www.zambiamonitor.com/zambias-nationwide-geological-survey-on-track-for-2026-completion-mines-ministry-confirms/)). KoBold's 2025 Congo deal swapped data digitisation for access ([Geomechanics.io](https://www.geomechanics.io/news/article/congobelgium-transfer-of-colonial-geological-records-key-points-for-mine-planners)). Saudi Ma'aden bought 9.9% of Ivanhoe Electric and formed a 50/50 exploration JV that uses Ivanhoe's technology ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2023-07-06/ivanhoe-electric-establishes-exploration-jv-saudi-arabian-mining-giant)). Fleet Space won a four-year Ma'aden contract through a JV with a Saudi partner, Tahreez ([Global Mining Review](https://www.globalminingreview.com/technology-digitalisation/06052025/maaden-signs-fleet-space-tahreez-jv-for-multi-year-exploration-contract/)), and took equity in junior Thor Energy in exchange for surveys ([Global Mining Review](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/05092023/fleet-space-and-thor-energy-partner-to-advance-mineral-exploration/amp/)). Adapted to Pakistan, the realistic options are a targeting JV with PMDC or OGDCL in which PakGeoLith takes a carried interest (a free share of the project), revenue-sharing on digitised state data, PPPs through the federal PPP Authority or GB's new PPP unit, and subcontracting to donor programmes. Startup support from Ignite's National Incubation Centers and tax incentives in Special Technology Zones can help with costs ([Ignite](https://ignite.org.pk/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Ignite-Business-Plan-2024-28-Final-Version.pdf); [STZA](https://www.stza.gov.pk/)). Contracts should be signed with each province separately, with federal bodies kept as conveners and data sources. Military-linked entities such as FWO can be customers or partners and can open doors. They may also worry Western investors, so PakGeoLith should plan for data-localisation and security-vetting demands.

Mine owners are most valuable as **sellers and data contributors**, not as subscribers. Mining companies and investors are the paying core, and end-product buyers are the demand that makes the marketplace worth joining.

## Channels and events: PMIF26 in nine days, then Riyadh, Cape Town and Toronto

**PMIF26 runs 12–13 November 2026 at the Jinnah Convention Centre, Islamabad.** It is government-organised, SIFC-endorsed, with OGDCL as founding partner and Wood Mackenzie producing the conference. Speakers include the Prime Minister, the Field Marshal, Barrick's John Thornton and Robert Friedland. There is a technical conference of more than 50 papers, a critical-minerals innovation workshop, a Mineral Expo and procurement briefings ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/28/pakistan-minerals-investment-forum-2026-pmif26-set-for-november-12-13-in-islamabad/)). The 2025 edition drew about 5,000 delegates from more than 50 countries and produced 16 MoUs ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=192465)). Exhibitors include PMDC, NRL, FWO, Barrick, PPL, GHPL and Reko Diq, and the academic partners are NUST, the University of Peshawar and UET Peshawar and Lahore. Nearly every target buyer and partner in this report will be in one building, nine days from now. A booth may be too late to book, but delegate passes, pre-booked meetings and a free "Pakistan tenure snapshot" giveaway are not. The forum has already moved once, from April ([APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1179098)), so dates should be checked again the day before.

After PMIF26, the international calendar suggests a sequence. **Future Minerals Forum in Riyadh (12–14 January 2027)** reaches Gulf investors, and Pakistan demonstrated its NMDC there last year ([Global Mining Review](https://globalminingreview.com/events/future-minerals-forum-2027/)). **Mining Indaba in Cape Town (8–11 February 2027)** follows ([Mining Indaba](https://miningindaba.com/register-your-interest)). **PDAC in Toronto (7–10 March 2027)** reaches juniors and the Canadian diaspora; Fleet Space used PDAC for product launches and a customer-led "ExoSphere & Beer" side event ([PDAC](https://pdac.ca/convention-2027/attending-2027); [Fleet Space](https://fleetspace.com/news/pdac-2024-fleet-spaces-view-from-the-heights-of-mineral-exploration)). For the marketplace, Tucson (AGTA GemFair 31 January–4 February 2027) and Hong Kong's March gem show reach international gem buyers ([InStore](https://instoremag.com/agta-gemfair-tucson-date-pattern-revealed-for-2027-2029/)). PGJDC's multi-city gem exhibitions reach Pakistani traders cheaply ([PGJDC](https://pgjdc.org/company-initiatives.php?catId=27)).

At home, the best association partners are those that speak for many small units with tenure problems. The **Frontier Mines Owners Association** in KP has said weak technology and training hold the sector back ([APP](https://www.app.com.pk/features/kps-marble-a-treasure-for-economic-prosperity/)). The **All Pakistan Marble Industries Association** has complained that lease transfers in Balochistan are difficult ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/86894)). Both sources are old, so current leadership needs checking. A free licence-status check offered through these associations would address a grievance they have already stated. University geology departments supply credibility, advisers and field ambassadors. Online, **YouTube (54.3M ad reach) and Facebook (52.9M) are the mass channels, and LinkedIn (18M, up 28.6%) is the professional one** ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-pakistan)). Facebook's Pakistani audience is about 75% male ([NapoleonCat](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/facebook-users-in-pakistan/2026/06/)), which matches mine owners and gem traders. A workable mix is Urdu and Pashto YouTube explainers ("how to check a mining lease"), Facebook lead ads that open a WhatsApp chat, and LinkedIn for investors and the diaspora. Credibility signals matter as much as reach: named reference customers (Fleet Space leans heavily on Rio Tinto and Barrick ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/?p=159446))), an advisory board with university faculty and a retired GSP or PMDC official, a Competent Person partner, and visible data timestamps from official cadastres. The website review also found **investment-advice language that conflicts with the site's disclaimers**. That should be rewritten into neutral "intelligence, not advice" language before foreign lawyers and investors read it.

## Risks, and what to verify before you join

The biggest risk is **positioning risk**. A founder who insists on a claim that a single link to Fleet Space at Reko Diq or SUPARCO's HS-1 can disprove will lose sophisticated buyers. How the founder responds to that evidence is worth testing directly. The second is **state substitution**. If the NMDC, provincial cadastres or HS-1 products become good and free, the atlas loses value unless PakGeoLith has become their analytics or distribution partner. The third is **cheap substitutes**: Farmonaut-style foreign vendors and local freelancers can undercut reports. The fourth is **jurisdiction and politics**. Provincial–federal disputes, the suspended Balochistan Act and the role of military-linked entities can freeze deals. The fifth is **security**: Balochistan saw more than half of Pakistan's attacks in 2025 with 6% of its population, and Barrick's security review pushed Reko Diq production from 2028 to 2029 ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/minerals-diplomacy-meets-market-reality-case-pakistan)). That limits ground-truthing and customer activity in the most important province. The sixth is **marketplace and legal risk**: handling money without an escrow law, tax withholding, liability if a listed seller's title is bad, and liability if a report reads as investment advice. The seventh is **report-quality risk**. The sample T4's same-day date, against a 20–30 day promise, and its broken formatting make clients suspect templated output. In a trust business, that is the product. Governance risks also apply: Pakistan ranks 136 of 182 on Transparency International's index, and the courts have a 2.4M-case backlog ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/minerals-diplomacy-meets-market-reality-case-pakistan)).

Before joining, ask for evidence on the points below. Answers that are vague or defensive are themselves a signal.

| Verify | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Paying customers and revenue since the September 2026 launch: how many reports sold, at what price, to whom; renewals | Shows whether anyone pays at current prices |
| Where the satellite data comes from (own satellites, or free Landsat/Sentinel/ASTER/EMIT), and who interprets it, with what qualifications | Decides whether the "unique system" is real, and whether reports would pass a Competent Person's review |
| Legal right to republish cadastre and GSP data, and the plan for Balochistan coverage | The core asset could be challenged or stay incomplete |
| Report process: actual turnaround, quality control, and why the sample T4 was dated the day inputs arrived | Trust is the product |
| Marketplace status: are auctions live, who holds buyer funds, and is that legally structured | Regulatory and fraud exposure |
| Runway, funding, cap table, your equity and vesting, salary, and decision rights | Your personal downside |
| Government relationships that actually exist (MoUs, meetings, names) versus aspirations | Government is the largest revenue pool and the slowest |
| Founder openness to repositioning and to percentage-based marketplace pricing | Shows whether the strategy in this report can be carried out |
| HS-1 operational status and data terms from SUPARCO; current status of NMDC and the PGJDC portal | Whether the state is a supplier or a competitor |
| Terms and disclaimers reviewed by a Pakistani lawyer, including the "investment advice" wording | Liability if a client loses money |

## Conclusion

The research changes the founder's question. PakGeoLith is not a satellite company that happens to sell in Pakistan. Its value is being the trusted local layer between Pakistan's fragmented mineral system and the money now arriving to exploit it. Satellites, maps and AI are tools anyone can buy or download. Clean, current, multi-province tenure and law data, with Balochistan included, plus reports that a foreign lawyer or lender will accept and a marketplace where licence checks, assay and escrow travel with every deal, are much harder to copy. They also give the state a reason to partner with PakGeoLith rather than compete with it. This makes the business slower to build than the founder may hope, and better protected once built.

For someone deciding whether to join, the opportunity is real and well timed. Pakistan's minerals push, foreign entrants with large information gaps, and the absence of a local integrated rival all favour PakGeoLith. But the company's next nine days say more than its pitch deck. A team that fixes its website, drops an over-claim, brings a clean sample report and a dollar price list to PMIF26, and comes away with pilots is one that can turn the idea into a business. A team that turns up claiming to be the only one in the world is likely to struggle.
