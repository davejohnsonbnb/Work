# PakGeoLith prices trust in two currencies

## Executive summary

**The bottom line.** PakGeoLith's old prices fit a consumer app, not a professional data and trust service. The subscriptions cost PKR 5,000–10,000 a year (about USD 18–36), a compliance report cost PKR 30,000 (about USD 107), and auctions charged a flat PKR 5,000 per lot. The cheapest global retail mining-data product, Opaxe Standard, costs USD 10 a month ([Opaxe](https://www.opaxe.com/)). Mining Intelligence starts at **USD 4,400 per user per year** ([Mining Intelligence](https://www2.miningintelligence.com/exploration)). UK automated geology and mining desktop reports cost **GBP 39–199 each** ([Groundsure](https://www.groundsure.com/products)). Online gem auctions take **12.5% from the seller plus 9% from the buyer** ([Catawiki](https://auction.catawiki.com/en/help/fees/what-is-the-buyer-protection-fee)). This report proposes two price lists for every product. The **USD international list** sits at or a little below global benchmarks and is for foreign companies, investors, DFIs and exporters. The **PKR local list** is for Pakistan-resident buyers. It runs at about 20% of the USD price for individuals, 30–40% for SMEs and reports, and 50–60% for corporates. Global SaaS sells in Pakistan at 15–40% of US list prices ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/10/10/chatgpt-go-brings-cheaper-gpt-5-access-to-pakistan/); [Spotify PK](https://www.spotify.com/pk/premium/)), which supports those ratios.

**What changes the most.**
- **Subscriptions** become a six-step ladder, from Free to Enterprise. Each tier gets a stated AI-assistant allowance, and premium map layers are sold as add-ons.
- **Point-geology tests and 5 km briefs** get their own prices, set using the UK's Groundsure as the benchmark.
- **The T1–T5 satellite surveys** get USD prices of USD 1,500–25,000+. Even the top tier costs much less than the airborne survey a client would otherwise have to fund.
- **Compliance screening** becomes a three-level product: a quick check, a standard report and a transaction-grade memo.
- **The marketplace** moves from flat fees to percentage commissions, escrow and pass-through charges. On a USD 50,000 chromite lot, platform revenue rises from about USD 18 to about USD 1,300.

**Who pays.** Money comes mainly from:
- domestic corporates and SOEs entering hard-rock mining;
- foreign juniors and investors;
- banks, DFIs and law firms;
- in time, provincial governments.

Mine owners matter most as **sellers and data contributors**. End buyers of minerals, such as steel, cement, ceramics and jewellery firms, are the demand that makes sellers join.

**What to do now.** PMIF26, the government's minerals investment forum, takes place in Islamabad on **12–13 November 2026**, nine days from today ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/28/pakistan-minerals-investment-forum-2026-pmif26-set-for-november-12-13-in-islamabad/)). PakGeoLith should arrive with:
- the USD price sheet;
- a clean sample report;
- an honest positioning line: "Pakistan's integrated mineral-intelligence platform", not "the only one in the world".

**Exchange rate used: USD 1 = PKR 280** for every conversion in this report. The interbank rate was about PKR 276.8 on 23 October 2026 ([PakTribune](https://paktribune.com/rupee-closes-at-276-82-per-dollar-after-another-marginal-gain/)), so PKR 280 includes a small buffer. Inflation is back above 10% ([pkrevenue.com](https://pkrevenue.com/pakistan-inflation-outlook-october-2026/)), so the PKR list should be reviewed every 6–12 months.

## 1. Sell trust, price twice, take a percentage

PakGeoLith brings together in one product:
- a geology and mineral-occurrence atlas;
- multi-province tenure data (who holds which licence);
- a mining-law register;
- an Urdu/English AI assistant;
- satellite targeting reports;
- a marketplace.

The defensible part is not the satellite imagery. Free NASA EMIT, Landsat, Sentinel and ASTER data are available to anyone ([JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-sensor-produces-first-global-maps-of-surface-minerals-in-arid-regions/)), and SUPARCO launched its own hyperspectral satellite in October 2025 ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1950017/pakistan-launches-its-first-hyperspectral-satellite)). What is hard to copy is the **cleaned tenure and law data, interpreted for a specific deal, from a platform that also verifies sellers and holds payments**.

Three pricing rules follow from this.
- **Price interpretation, not lookups.** A raw registry search is almost free worldwide: Western Australia charges about AUD 10.60 ([WA DMIRS](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-07/minerals_fees_charges2025-26.pdf)) and Punjab land records charge PKR 900 ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/07/08/punjab-increases-fees-for-land-record-services/)). Interpretation is where the value is, and law-firm title opinions start at about USD 5,000 ([forum estimate, weak](https://gohaynesvilleshale.com/xn/detail/2117179:Comment:2588634)).
- **Price foreign and corporate clients in USD.** The rupee has been stable, but inflation is above 10% and the State Bank raised the policy rate to 12% on 27 October 2026 ([pkrevenue.com](https://pkrevenue.com/pakistan-inflation-outlook-october-2026/)). USD export revenue may also qualify for the 0.25% final tax on IT exports, which runs until June 2029 ([CW Pakistan](https://cwpakistan.com/budget-2026-27-extends-it-sector-final-tax-regime-three-years-until-june-2029/)). A tax adviser needs to confirm whether PakGeoLith's reports count as IT-enabled services.
- **Earn a percentage only where we carry risk or do work.** Verification, assay coordination and escrow justify a commission. A plain listing does not.

| Revenue engine | Who pays most | Pricing logic | When it pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reports (briefs, compliance, T1–T5 surveys) | Corporates, foreign juniors, lenders, law firms | Per item; USD list ≈ global, PKR list ≈ 30–40% | Now |
| Subscriptions + AI + layers | Professionals, investors, teams | Per seat per year, AI allowance included | 0–6 months |
| Law register | Law firms, banks, compliance teams | Per organisation per year | 0–6 months |
| Enterprise / API | Banks, DFIs, majors, consultancies | Annual contract | 6–12 months |
| Government licences / portals | Provincial mines departments, SIFC/PMIF, GSP | Annual licence under PPRA thresholds; portals by tender | 6–18 months |
| Marketplace | Licensed sellers and buyers | Membership + commission + escrow + pass-throughs | 9–18 months, after escrow partner |

## 3. The complete price list, item by item

### Pricing rules that apply to everything

- **Currency.** Foreign buyers, multinationals and exporters pay the **USD list**. Pakistan-resident individuals, SMEs and corporates pay the **PKR list**. All conversions use PKR 280.
- **Taxes.** PKR prices **exclude provincial sales tax on services**: 16% in Punjab and ICT, 15% in Sindh and KP, with reduced IT rates possibly applying ([ConSerIC](https://conseric.pk/updates-punjab-sales-tax-2026-27/); [lookuptax](https://lookuptax.com/docs/country/pakistan)). Commission income attracts **12% Section 233 withholding for filers** ([PKRevenue](https://pkrevenue.com/advance-tax-rates-on-brokerage-commission-for-2025-26/)).
- **Indexation.** Multi-year PKR contracts carry a CPI escalator of about 10% a year (inference).
- **Billing.** Annual billing gets two months free. Payment by debit card, Raast, IBFT or bank transfer. Only about 4% of Pakistan's 61M payment cards are credit cards ([SBP](https://archive.sbp.org.pk/press/2025/Pr-30-Dec-2025.pdf)).

The working assumption is about **4,000 tokens per answer** that draws on law text or briefs (assumption, not sourced). On that basis, 500k tokens is about 125 questions and 2M is about 500. The default model is a low-cost "mini-class" model, which costs PakGeoLith about **USD 0.78 (PKR 215) per 1M tokens**. A premium model costs about USD 4.40 per 1M (inference from [AnotherWrapper](https://anotherwrapper.com/llm-pricing/claude-sonnet-5) prices), so premium-model use **counts at 5x** against the allowance. Hard caps apply. Even at Team level, the AI cost stays below 25% of the price.

## 7. Conclusion

The repricing is about more than charging more. It turns PakGeoLith from a cheap map subscription into a **trust service whose price follows the size of the decision it supports**. A PKR 2,500 point test helps someone decide whether a site is worth a visit. A USD 12,000 survey helps a company decide where to send a drilling budget worth hundreds of thousands. A 1% commission with escrow decides whether a USD 50,000 chromite deal happens at all. Running two currency lists lets the same product serve a Swat gem trader and a Toronto junior without undercutting either. Moving marketplace fees from a flat charge to percentages means PakGeoLith earns only when it carries risk, which also makes it harder for buyers and sellers to cut the platform out.

The weakest parts of this list are the parts with no benchmark: government portals, the Balochistan layer, and the T3–T5 USD prices, because global vendors hide their report prices. These should be tested at PMIF26 and in the first pilots rather than defended as fixed. Publishing USD prices at all is unusual in a quote-only industry, and could become a marketing advantage in its own right, provided the sample report is good enough to justify the price.
