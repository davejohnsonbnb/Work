# Pakistan Mining Sector: Market Structure and Customer Segments for PakGeoLith

Research date: 3 November 2026. All figures are dated where the source allows. Research was limited to about 17 search/fetch calls, so several items (per-mineral PBS export totals, provincial lease counts outside KP, named small-operator associations) remain gaps and are listed as such.

## 1. How big is the sector? (GDP share, exports by mineral, licences, small operators)

### Takeaway
Mining is a small but politically prioritised sector: about 2.5–3.2% of GDP, about $2–3bn a year in mineral exports (90% raw), about 5,000 operating mines, and an estimated 400,000–515,000 artisanal and small-scale miners. Copper (Saindak-linked ingots to China) is the only export line above $1bn. Gemstones are tiny in formal trade (about $6–7m) but government-targeted at $0.5–1bn. KP alone has 4,917 mineral titles (October 2026), so all four provinces together plausibly hold well over 10,000 titles.

### Cited Findings
**GDP and aggregate exports**
- In January 2026 the Planning Minister said mineral exports are a "modest" $2bn and that mining contributes "only 2-3 per cent to the GDP". He also cited about 5,000 operational mines, about 68.5m tonnes a year of production, 40% of land geologically mapped, 90% of exports raw or unprocessed, an export potential of $6–8bn a year this decade, and copper ore exports to China of about $1.2bn a year (Pak-China Mineral Cooperation Forum, 28 January 2026) — [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1969522)
- ICMA says mining contributes less than 3% of GDP and that mineral exports could rise from about $3bn to $8bn. It puts reserves at about $8tn across about 600,000 sq km, with 92 known minerals of which 52 are commercially exploited (October 2026) — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/16/pakistan-the-next-critical-minerals-destination/)
- An OGDCL report likewise cites expected growth in mineral exports from $3bn to $8bn — [ARY News](https://arynews.tv/pakistan-aims-to-raise-mineral-exports-to-8-billion-ogdc-report-says)
- Mining contributes 3.2% of GDP and about 0.1% of global mineral exports — [Friday Times, April 2025](https://thefridaytimes.com/12-Apr-2025/unlocking-pakistan-s-mineral-wealth-ambitious-rhetoric-or-real-economic-transformation). One commentary site claims 21% of GDP, which conflicts with every other source and should be disregarded — [IIPS](https://iips.com.pk/?p=9338)
- Mining GDP was PKR 144,267m in Q3 2025, up from PKR 143,283m in Q2 2025 — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/pakistan/gdp-from-mining)
- Conflict: aggregate exports are cited as $2bn (Planning Minister) versus $3bn (ICMA/OGDCL). The difference is probably whether refined copper or metals are counted.

**Exports by mineral**
- Copper: exports to China passed $1bn in the first eight months of FY2025-26 ($1.02bn in copper ingots plus $280m in other copper products), per customs data (May 2026) — [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/05/25/pakistans-copper-exports-to-china-cross-1-billion-in-8-months-amid-surge-in-processed-metal-trade); Business Recorder reports $1.30bn, with the period unclear — [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40422690). Copper ores and concentrates to China were only about $11m in calendar 2025 (China customs, via search summary) — [Aaj](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330429078)
- Chromite: chromium ores and concentrates to China totalled $89.43m in calendar 2025. In H1 2025 they halved to $31.58m on weak stainless-steel demand — [Aaj / APP summaries](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330429078). The TDAP chromite potential analysis is from 2022 — [TDAP](https://tdap.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Analysis-of-Chromite-Potential-Final-2.pdf)
- Marble: marble cut into blocks or slabs exported to China (HS 25151200) was $5.41m in 2025 against $5.02m in 2024. Crude granite fell while cut granite rose. Pakistan is described as China's third-largest supplier of construction stone (February 2026) — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/02/10/pakistans-becomes-chinas-3rd-largest-supplier-of-construction-stone-material/amp/)
- Gemstones: formal exports are about $5.8m a year against claimed reserves of about $450bn. The first National Gemstone Policy targets $1bn in exports within five years — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2628746/amp). APP describes the National Gemstone Policy 2026–2030 target as $500m–$1bn — [APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1263616). A PM-appointed committee put potential at over $2bn a year against about $7m recorded — [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/amp/40393641)
- Rare earths and critical minerals: the first shipment of enriched REE and critical minerals went to US Strategic Metals on 2 October 2025 — [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1969522); [Marine Insight](https://www.marineinsight.com/shipping-news/pakistan-sends-first-ever-rare-earth-shipment-to-u-s-under-500-million-deal/)
- Antimony: deposits were discovered in Balochistan, and OGDCL and PMDC plan a joint exploration venture — [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=191118)
- Reko Diq (future): feasibility study completed February 2025, with first production by end-2028. Phase 1 is about 240kt copper and 297koz gold a year, rising to an average of 460kt copper and 520koz gold over the first 10 years after the Phase 2 expansion in 2034. Total capex is $8.83bn and first-year exports are projected at $2.8bn — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/16/pakistan-the-next-critical-minerals-destination/)

**Licences, mines and small operators**
- KP mineral licences and titles: 482 from 1947 to 2010, 1,762 by 2020, 3,962 by February 2024, and 4,917 now (4,133 in settled districts, 784 in merged districts). More than 500 new applications are pending, and FWO holds one licence (Khawaja Asif, 17 October 2026) — [ARY News](https://arynews.tv/khawaja-asif-denies-military-seeking-mineral-licences-in-kp)
- KP mine owners say 765 mining areas are inaccessible or restricted after forest boundaries were expanded. They are seeking a review of a 400% increase in annual surface rent (Mine Owners Association, Hazara, July 2026) — [APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1245381)
- Balochistan's Mines Department listed 144 idle mining leases for action and is digitising its royalty system and title applications — [Balochistan MMDD](https://mmd.balochistan.gov.pk/?p=24); [MMDD](https://mmd.balochistan.gov.pk/?p=108)
- 47 illegal chromite mines were closed in Pishin, Balochistan — [APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1150536)
- ASM: about 515,000 artisanal miners (30,000 in gold), from 2014 data published in 2018; earlier estimates were about 400,000 (2013) and 230,000 (1999). Mine counts are 2,400–3,250, with 5–20% illegal — [artisanalmining.org](https://artisanalmining.org/InventoryData/doku.php/country:pakistan)
- Title types include reconnaissance and exploration licences, retention licences, large- and small-scale mining leases, and gemstone permits, all granted by provincial licensing authorities. Balochistan and KP enacted new mining laws in 2025 — [Mondaq comparative guide](https://www.mondaq.com/energy-and-natural-resources/1740300/mining-comparative-guide); small-scale mining provisions — [Balochistan Minerals Rules 2002](https://mmd.balochistan.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/balochistan-minerals-rules-2002.pdf)
- About 300,000 people are directly or indirectly supported by the gemstone trade in KP. Namak Mandi (Peshawar) has had a gem market since 1970, and APCEA members exported about $4m in 2021-22 — [APP](https://www.app.com.pk/features/gemstones-a-valuable-asset-for-revenue-generation/)

### Inferences
- With about 4,900 KP titles plus Balochistan, Punjab (Salt Range, coal, gypsum, limestone), Sindh (Thar coal, limestone) and GB (gemstones), a national title universe of roughly 10,000–15,000 is plausible but unverified. That is the addressable base for PKR 30k per-licence compliance reports.
- Fast title growth in KP (482 to 4,917 in about 15 years) together with 500 or more pending applications points to a busy secondary market in transfers and joint ventures. That is where title verification has the most value.
- The investable story is concentrated: Reko Diq and Chagai copper-gold, Saindak, chromite, REE/antimony and gemstones. Construction minerals (marble, limestone, gypsum, coal) have many more operators but low value per title.

### Gaps
- No PBS FY2025 or FY2026 national export totals were found for rock salt (Himalayan salt), marble and granite, gypsum, coal, iron ore or lithium. The Economic Survey 2025-26 mining chapter was not retrieved. The PBS external trade tables and Economic Survey chapter 3 should be checked.
- No official lease or licence counts were found for Balochistan, Punjab, Sindh or GB.
- No current (post-2018) ASM headcount was found.

## 2. Customer segments, named organisations and pain points

### Takeaway
There are six commercially distinct segments: (a) small licence holders and mine owners, the largest group and the most price-sensitive; (b) domestic corporates and SOEs now moving into hard-rock minerals (OGDCL, PPL, Mari, PMDC, FWO, Fatima/Globacore); (c) foreign majors and state investors (Barrick, Chinese SOEs at Saindak and Duddar, Manara, USSM, Nova Minerals); (d) buyers and traders (Chinese importers, gemstone dealers); (e) financiers and DFIs (IFC/World Bank, ADB, US EXIM), plus law, consulting and insurance firms; (f) government, academia and NGOs. Common pain points are title opacity and fraud, discretionary licensing, scarce digital geodata (only 40% mapped), forest and access restrictions, security, and weak certification and price discovery for gemstones.

### Cited Findings
**(b) Domestic corporates and SOEs entering minerals**
- Mari Minerals (a subsidiary of Mari Energies) took an 87.5% operated stake in Chagai licences covering about 40 sq km — [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=186585). It also entered a JV with IRH Mining and BMRL for a 25% interest in EL302 and EL303 in Chagai — [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=188529). It is transferring a 49% working interest to Globacore Minerals (linked to Fatima Fertilizer, December 2025) — [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=221792)
- PPL received conditional approval for exploration licence EL-331 for precious and base metals in Chagai — [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=216856); PPL deal for a Balochistan mining project (July 2026) — [The Standard](https://thestandard.com.pk/pakistan-petroleum-inks-deal-to-develop-mining-project-in-balochistan/)
- OGDCL is a Reko Diq partner and is planning an antimony JV with PMDC — [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=191118)
- NRL discovered copper-gold mineralisation in Chagai — [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40356502/nrl-discovers-copper-gold-mineralization-in-chagai)
- FWO signed two MoUs with USSM (8 September 2025) and holds a KP licence (Boya, North Waziristan) — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/16/pakistan-the-next-critical-minerals-destination/); [ARY](https://arynews.tv/khawaja-asif-denies-military-seeking-mineral-licences-in-kp)

**(c) Foreign majors and investors**
- Reko Diq ownership: Barrick 50%, Balochistan 25%, three federal SOEs 25% — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/16/pakistan-the-next-critical-minerals-destination/)
- Saudi Manara Minerals (a Ma'aden–PIF JV) has been in talks for a stake in Reko Diq. 2024 reports put it at 15% of the federal 25%, valued at about $540m or $500m–$1bn. No completed deal was found as of November 2026 — [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/web/manara-may-invest-in-pakistans-reko-diq-mine-minister-says/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/saudi-arabia-eyes-stake-in-pakistans-mining-project-1.1724841341402)
- US Strategic Metals (Missouri): first-phase investment of about $500m, with MoUs on processing and logistics — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/16/pakistan-the-next-critical-minerals-destination/); [ARY](https://arynews.tv/us-pakistan-sign-mou-on-critical-minerals-resources)
- Nova Minerals (Nasdaq and ASX listed) has expressed strong interest in investing in Pakistan (November 2025) — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/11/14/us-based-firm-eyes-investment-in-pakistans-mining-sector/amp/)
- China is described as the central partner, through the Saindak copper-gold, Duddar lead-zinc and Thar coal projects — [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1969522)
- PMIF25 (8–9 April 2025) drew 16 MoUs, 5,000 delegates and more than 50 countries. PMIF26 was rescheduled to 12–13 November 2026 at the Jinnah Convention Centre, Islamabad — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/16/pakistan-the-next-critical-minerals-destination/); Pakistan is pushing for US investment in Balochistan minerals (October 2026) — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2635302/govt-seeks-us-investment-in-minerals)

**(e) Financiers and DFIs**
- The Reko Diq financing package is over $2.6bn: IFC and World Bank $700m (June 2025), ADB $410m ($300m loans plus a $110m guarantee for Balochistan's equity, August 2025), and US EXIM $1.25bn (December 2025) — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/16/pakistan-the-next-critical-minerals-destination/)

**(f) Government and public data (partner, competitor or customer)**
- The National Minerals Data Centre is a PSDP project of the Petroleum Division (Mineral Wing) with GSP and the provinces, budgeted at Rs 295m, with Rs 150.56m allocated in FY23 — [Pakistan Today](https://www.pakistantoday.com.pk/2023/01/15/rs150-560-mln-being-spent-to-advance-national-minerals-data-centre-project); [Pak Observer](https://pakobserver.net/national-minerals-data-centre-being-es-tablished-for-investors-facilitation-projects-planning/)
- KP runs an online mining cadastre portal — [KP Cadastre](https://portal.kpminerals.gov.pk)
- A National Minerals Harmonisation Framework aims to standardise rules across provinces — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2538920/pakistan-bets-on-minerals-for-revival)
- GSP mineral research labs were upgraded to attract foreign investment — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2619264/amp)

**Pain points (evidence)**
- Title and licensing integrity: Herald reports allegations of ministerial interference in Balochistan's mining committee and of leases transferred without informing the original holders. The World Bank recommended removing discretionary licensing powers. This rests on a single anonymous source — [Herald/Dawn](https://herald.dawn.com/news/1152909/everything-around-here-is-mine)
- NAB has pursued Balochistan mines department officials and a coal company owner over losses to the exchequer (dates unclear) — [vLex](https://pk.vlex.com/vid/deputy-director-among-two-820842841)
- Access and compliance costs: 765 restricted sites in KP and a 400% surface-rent rise — [APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1245381)
- Data scarcity: only 40% of land is geologically mapped — [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1969522)
- Gemstones: weak certification, limited cutting capacity, smuggling and raw exports — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2628746/amp); [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/amp/40393641)
- Illegal mining (47 chromite mines closed in Pishin) — [APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1150536)

### Inferences
- Segment (b) is the most promising early enterprise buyer. Oil and gas E&Ps (Mari, PPL, OGDCL) and fertiliser groups (Fatima) have cash, are new to hard-rock minerals, and are actively farming in and out of Chagai licences. Due diligence on title chains and surrounding tenure is directly useful to them.
- Lucky Cement, Fauji (FFC, Fauji Cement), Saindak's operator (MCC Resources) and Duddar's operator (Hunan/China Nonferrous) were not confirmed in sources this pass. Cement groups hold limestone and clay leases and are plausible compliance-report and tenure-monitoring buyers.
- Foreign juniors and US entrants (USSM, Nova Minerals and others attracted by PMIF26) face the steepest information gap and are used to paying for data. Satellite survey reports at PKR 150k–1m (about $540–3,600) are cheap by international standards for this group.
- DFIs, EXIM and their advisers (environmental and social, legal) need ESG, ASM and tenure-conflict mapping around financed projects. They are institutional subscription or bespoke-report buyers, with PMIF26 as an immediate sales venue.

### Gaps
- No named Chinese importers of Pakistani chromite or copper and no named Peshawar or Gilgit gem dealer firms were found, beyond the APCEA association.
- Insurers, law firms (for example the authors of the Mondaq guide) and consultants were not individually researched.
- No sources were found on KYC pain for mineral buyers specifically.

## 3. Which segments buy which product, and willingness or ability to pay

### Takeaway
A tiered fit: small licence holders buy low-ticket compliance reports and basic subscriptions, but are price-sensitive and offline. Domestic corporates and foreign investors buy satellite exploration reports and premium subscriptions. Financiers, law firms and consultants buy the law register and due-diligence reports. Traders and gem dealers are the marketplace side, but formal gem trade is tiny, so trust infrastructure (certification, KYC) comes before volume.

### Cited Findings
- The scale of capital and finance being deployed (Reko Diq at $8.83bn capex, a $2.6bn financing package, USSM at about $500m) shows institutional buyers can absorb report costs in the PKR 150k–1m range — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/16/pakistan-the-next-critical-minerals-destination/)
- Small KP owners are already complaining about a 400% surface-rent increase, which indicates cost sensitivity — [APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1245381)
- Formal gem exports are about $5.8–7m against $2bn or more of claimed potential, with certification and smuggling the key barriers — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2628746/amp); [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/amp/40393641)

### Inferences (product-to-segment fit matrix; not directly sourced)
| Segment | Subscription (PKR 5–10k/yr) | Compliance report (PKR 30k) | Satellite survey (PKR 150k–1m) | Law register | Marketplace | Ability to pay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small mine owners and licence holders (KP 4,917 titles; national roughly 10–15k est.) | Medium (basic tier) | High (renewals, rent, disputes, sale or transfer) | Low–medium (only before a sale or JV) | Low | High (finding buyers) | Low |
| Domestic corporates and SOEs (Mari, PPL, OGDCL, PMDC, FWO, Fatima/Globacore, cement groups) | High (team seats) | High (bulk, portfolio) | High | High | Medium (offtake) | High |
| Foreign majors and juniors (Barrick, Manara, USSM, Nova, Chinese SOEs) | High | Medium | High (target generation, area-selection) | High | Low–medium | Very high |
| Buyers and traders (Chinese importers, domestic industry, gem dealers) | Medium | Medium (supplier title KYC) | Low | Low | High | Medium |
| DFIs, banks, insurers, law and consulting firms | High (institutional) | High (diligence on collateral and title) | Medium | Very high | Low | High |
| Universities, NGOs, government | Low–medium (discounted) | Low | Low | Medium | None | Low (grant-funded) |

- A compliance report at PKR 30k is about 3–6 years of subscription. For small owners it should be framed around a transaction event (sale, transfer, bank loan, dispute) rather than as routine.
- Indicative sizing: if 5–10% of an estimated 10–15k titles buy one PKR 30k report a year, that is about PKR 15–45m a year. That points to enterprise and foreign segments carrying the revenue.

### Gaps
- No primary willingness-to-pay data (surveys or comparable Pakistani data products) was found.
- No PKR prices for competing services, such as consultants' title searches or GSP data sales, were found.

## 4. Overseas Pakistani and diaspora interest

### Takeaway
The state is courting diaspora investment into mining rhetorically (the Overseas Pakistanis Convention, "brain gain" framing), but no data quantifies diaspora mining investment. The segment is speculative: likely small-ticket investors who need remote title verification and trustworthy partners, which fits subscriptions plus compliance reports.

### Cited Findings
- Commentary on the Overseas Pakistanis Convention says the diaspora is invited to invest in sectors including mining, with digital platforms promised to ease investment (opinion piece) — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/perspective/pakistan-shifts-from-brain-drain-to-brain-gain/)
- Some segments of the diaspora have criticised the minerals push, questioning government motives and capacity — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2538920/pakistan-bets-on-minerals-for-revival)
- Government roadshows target Saudi Arabia, China, the US and Australia (Sydney conference), which are not diaspora-specific — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2619927)

### Inferences
- Remote diaspora investors are the group most exposed to title fraud, since they cannot visit sites or provincial offices. A "verify before you invest" licence report is a natural product for them, marketed through Gulf, UK and North American channels and Roshan Digital Account-type routes (unverified).

### Gaps
- No figures were found on diaspora capital flowing into mining, or on diaspora-owned mining companies. SBP and SIFC data were not retrieved.
